# Skill: honest scope — never promise what the club cannot deliver

Outreach carries the club's name to strangers. An overpromise becomes a commitment
someone has to honour later, and the club is small. Honesty is the product.

**What you may state, and only if it is true and checkable:**
- Code.SCRIET is the official coding club of SCRIET, CCSU Meerut, and runs events
  (DSA contests, workshops, a hackathon, quiz/certificate projects). These are
  verifiable on `https://codescriet.dev/events`.
- We can offer: branding on `https://codescriet.dev` and event posters, a named
  workshop or contest, a short session slot for the sponsor's team, logo/mention on
  digital certificates, social features, and hiring access to members.

**What you must never state or imply:**
- Any member count, reach, follower or engagement number, or "past sponsors" list
  unless it is approved and sourced. Unknown number -> ask the Club department or
  escalate; never guess, never round, never borrow from a similar club.
- Free subscriptions, credits, swag, prizes, cash, or dates the *sponsor* would
  provide. Ask whether their program covers them; do not promise amounts or terms.
- Anything that commits the club: exclusivity, deliverables, logos shipped, dates
  locked, payment. These are Owner-level and go to the CEO.

**Hedged phrasing that stays honest.** Instead of "we have 800 members" write
"our members run weekly DSA sessions and ship real projects" (an activity claim you
can point to). Instead of "you'll get 1000 impressions" write "your branding goes
on the session material, the certificates, and the event page".

**When unsure, name the uncertainty.** "We can confirm exact figures and past
partners before a call" is honest and keeps the door open; an invented number that
later fails verification costs the club its credibility. Draft honesty is a gate:
`email_gate.py` blocks hype and unverified claims, and anything touching money or
terms is escalated, not drafted.
