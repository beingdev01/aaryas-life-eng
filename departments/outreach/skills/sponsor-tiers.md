# Skill: sponsor tiers and what a club can actually offer

Four tiers, and you rotate across them so no tier goes stale:

- **large** — big platforms (a cloud, a well-known IDE, a testing giant). They
  rarely give cash to a student club, but they run programs: campus communities,
  student packs, credits, swag, a session led by an engineer. Aim at the *program*,
  not the P&L. Their "yes" is a named initiative, not a cheque.
- **startup / dev-tools** — the best fit. They want developer mindshare and have
  DevRel or community teams who can say yes to a workshop, credits, or contest
  prizes fast. Route to a community / developer-advocacy address.
- **local (Meerut / NCR)** — businesses, coaching institutes, small agencies. They
  value hiring access and local visibility; a talk, a naming slot, or goodies at an
  event is realistic. Keep it concrete and in-city.

What Code.SCRIET can *actually* give, and the only things you may offer:
branding on `https://codescriet.dev` and on event posters; naming a workshop or
contest; a short session slot for the sponsor's team; logo and mention on digital
certificates; social features; and hiring access to members. That is the whole
catalogue. Credits, swag and prizes come *from* the sponsor's own programs — you
ask whether their program covers them, you do not promise amounts.

Working example — tier reasoning for a database company:
"Its developer tier lists free cloud credits for students, so the realistic ask
is a named database workshop plus a credits link for members, not a cash
sponsorship." That is tier-appropriate; "sponsor our annual fest for Rs. X" is not.
